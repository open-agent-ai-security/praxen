Executive Summary
Agent Remit (as declared)
FinBot is CineFlow Productions' invoice-processing assistant: it reviews vendor-submitted invoices and produces an approve, reject, or route-to-human decision with stated reasoning and a confidence score, within operator-configured thresholds and fraud controls. It is authorized to analyse invoices, read and write invoice status, hold vendor onboarding records, read and write its own configuration and natural-language goals, and notify vendors at their registered contact address. Its counterparties are authenticated CineFlow finance admins and registered vendors acting only on their own data, and the admin console must be authenticated, role-restricted, and unreachable from a public network. It must never disburse funds, never auto-approve at or above the manual-review threshold or when injection is detected, and never let vendor-supplied content redefine its goals, thresholds, or fraud state.
Behavior Summary (as observed)
The dominant pattern is a financial decision agent whose every declared safeguard exists as description rather than enforcement. Thresholds, a confidence requirement, and injection screening are all configured and displayed, but _approve_invoice commits an approval and sets payment_processed without consulting any of them, confidence_threshold has no consumer anywhere in the codebase, and the regex detector only records a boolean. Sitting on top of that is a full persistence chain: an unauthenticated POST /api/admin/finbot/goals writes arbitrary natural language into custom_goals, which is concatenated into the system prompt on every subsequent invocation under a header instructing the model to let it override the safety goals above — so one anonymous request permanently redirects every future decision, with no actor recorded and no alert raised.
Scope of Analysis
Python Flask application backed by SQLAlchemy over SQLite, deployed publicly on Render behind gunicorn. A single FinBotAgent class in src/services/finbot_agent.py drives OpenAI function-calling over five invoice tools, with a rule-based fallback engine (_fallback_processing, _analyze_business_context) that takes over silently whenever the OpenAI client fails to initialise. Three Flask blueprints — vendor, admin, and a vestigial user CRUD — are all registered under /api with no authentication middleware anywhere in the tree, and the admin console is linked from the public landing page. The agent's goals live in a natural-language custom_goals database column that is concatenated into the system prompt on every run, and vendor-authored invoice descriptions reach the model context raw as tool results; no notification channel, no logging framework, and no tests exist in the workspace.

Every score above is based on this scale. A score is a snapshot of observable posture — not a verdict on the people or team behind the system.

Score Label Meaning
5 Exemplary Best-in-class; automated, continuously tested, reference quality. Rarely achieved in shipping systems.
4 Strong Comprehensive controls, active management, minor gaps. Production-ready.
3 Established Documented controls consistently applied; known gaps accepted. A respectable baseline.
2 Partial Some controls exist but coverage is incomplete; key gaps remain.
1 Ad hoc Informal or inconsistent measures; relies on individual judgment.
